# learn-taichi-with-phoebe - source + coverage map

"Before your first class" - principles, history, philosophy and evidence for curious adults.
6 sessions, single track, principles-ladder arc. Running project = the learner's
practice-readiness kit. Signature interactive: taichi-live.js rooting lab.
Research verified 2026-08-06.

## The standing rail (on every page)

**This course prepares you for a class. It is not a class.** A form is transmitted
body-to-body; no web page can teach it. What text CAN carry is what this course teaches:
what the practice is, where it actually came from, the principles you can understand before
you can perform, what the evidence really shows, and how to find a teacher.

Claim labels, carried from the TCM course:
- **The text says** - a classical source, named and dated.
- **Tradition holds** - later systematization or school convention, NOT classical.
- **Research says** - a named study with exact numbers and certainty rating.

Honest inversion worth stating in Session 5: unlike most traditional practices, tai chi's
"research says" column is often the STRONG card. Say so plainly - the rail cuts both ways.

## Verified fact sheet (exact - use as written)

### Session 1: what it is + the history

**The name.** 太极拳 tàijíquán = 太 supreme + 极 ultimate/pole + 拳 fist/boxing. "Supreme
Ultimate Boxing." 太极 is cosmological and long predates the art - first in the 繫辭 Xìcí
commentary to the 易经: 易有太极，是生两仪 "the Changes has the Supreme Ultimate; this
generates the two modes."
- **CORRECTION worth teaching:** in English "tai chi" means the exercise; in Chinese 太极
  alone is the concept and the art is 太极拳 (with 拳).
- **CORRECTION:** the "chi" in "tai chi" is NOT the "chi" of qi. 极 jí (Wade-Giles *chi*) vs
  气 qì (Wade-Giles *ch'i*) - different characters. Pinyin is what journals use.

**Three things at once, genuinely in tension.** UNESCO holds all three: "relaxed, circular
movements that work in concert with breath regulation and the cultivation of a righteous and
neutral mind," structured on 五步 five steps, 八法 eight techniques, routines, and 推手 push
hands, "influenced by Daoist and Confucian thought and theories of traditional Chinese
medicine." NCCIH: "Tai chi originated as an ancient martial art in China. Over the years, it
has become more focused on health promotion and rehabilitation." Harvard uses "meditation in
motion" and the pun "medication in motion."
- The tension is historically real: pedagogy was reshaped in early-20th-century China toward
  fitness-as-nation-building (Jingwu Association c.1910, Central Guoshu Institute 1928).
- Peter Wayne's eight "active ingredients" (Harvard) - awareness, intention, structural
  integration, active relaxation, strengthening/flexibility, natural breathing, social
  support, embodied spirituality: "more like a multidrug combination." A framework, not a finding.

**内家/外家 internal vs external - NOT ancient.** First appearance: **1669**, 黄宗羲 Huang
Zongxi's 《王征南墓志铭》 tomb inscription for Wang Zhengnan (1617-1669).
> 少林以拳勇名天下，然主於搏人，人亦得以乘之。有所謂內家者，以靜制動，犯者應手即仆，故別少林為外家。

"Shaolin is world-famous for boxing prowess, but it emphasises attacking, so an opponent can
exploit that. There is what is called the internal school, which uses stillness to control
movement; an assailant is felled the instant the hand meets him. Shaolin is therefore classed
apart as the external school."
- **The epitaph is NOT about taijiquan** - taijiquan is not named in it. The link was made
  retroactively in the 20th century, partly by substituting the famous 王宗岳 Wang Zongyue for
  the obscure 王宗 Wang Zong of the epitaph.
- **Henning 1994: the split was political, not technical.** "The 'external' school and Shaolin
  Monastery represented foreign Buddhism, which symbolized the Manchu aggressors, while the
  'internal' school and Zhang Sanfeng represented indigenous Taoism." Huang was an anti-Manchu
  Ming loyalist; he dated Wang's life by the 60-year cycle to avoid Qing reign titles. "An act
  of political defiance rather than one of technical classification."
- Modern taiji+xingyi+bagua "internal" grouping dates to **1928**, Central Guoshu Institute
  (Nanjing), which split its faculty into a Shaolin division and a Wudang division.
- **Wile's periodisation, the best one-line teaching device:** "For General Qi Jiguang in the
  Ming, the basic binary in martial arts was 'flowery' versus 'practical'; for Huang Zongxi in
  the Ming-Qing Transition, it was 'internal' versus 'external'; in the Republican Period, it
  was Shaolin versus Wudang; and today, it is 'traditional' versus 'modern.'"

**Zhang Sanfeng 张三丰 - legend, not history.** Wile: "there is no record of a Zhang Sanfeng in
the Song Dynasty, and there is no mention in the Ming histories of any connection between the
immortal and the martial arts." Wile 2017 notes 81 different traditions of his creation, placing
him in Song, Yuan OR Ming, with three ways of writing his name.
- **THE BEST HONESTY BEAT IN THE COURSE:** earliest datable attribution of *taijiquan* to Zhang
  Sanfeng is **1867**, in 李亦畬 Li Yiyu's "three old manuscripts": 太极拳始自宋张三丰
  "Taijiquan began with Zhang Sanfeng of the Song dynasty." **Li Yiyu retracted it himself.**
  His **1881** manuscript given to 郝和 Hao He says: 太极拳不知始自何人，其精微巧妙，王宗岳论
  详且尽矣 - "I do not know with whom taijiquan began; its subtleties are exhaustively described
  in Wang Zongyue's treatise." **Same author, 14 years apart.**
- Henning's three phases: pre-1669 Zhang is a Daoist immortal with no martial connection;
  post-1669 originator of "internal" boxing; post-1900 originator of taijiquan specifically.
- First openly published link: 《太极拳经》 (1912), ed. 关百益 Guan Baiyi - about 60 years after
  taijiquan reached Beijing.
- **The snake-and-crane story has no verified pre-modern source.** The 1669 epitaph contains a
  DREAM: 夜夢玄帝授之拳法 "that night in a dream, the Dark Emperor transmitted the boxing method
  to him." Wile lists the animal story among folk narratives resting on "an immortal's dream,
  observation of a snake and stork, or the discovery of a secret manual in a cave, complete with
  fog machines and psychedelic light shows."

**The documented history.** 陈家沟 Chen Village, 温县 Wen County, Henan. UNESCO's own text:
taijiquan "originated in mid-seventeenth century in Wenxian County, Henan Province."
- 陈王廷 Chen Wangting, 9th-generation Chen, credited synthesiser. **HEDGE the dates** - three
  sets circulate: 1597-1664 (Wile, best-sourced), 1580-1660, c.1600-1680. Say "17th century."
- Likely technical source: Ming general 戚继光 Qi Jiguang's 《拳经》. Textual overlap was Tang
  Hao's key argument.
- Chain: 陈长兴 **Chen Changxing (1771-1853)** → 杨露禅 **Yang Luchan (1799-1872)**, who began
  study in **1820**, returned to 永年 Yongnian, and by **1850** was teaching the Imperial Guards'
  Palace Battalion in Beijing until his death.
- **唐豪 Tang Hao (1887-1959 per Wile; some Chinese sources 1897)** created the modern consensus.
  Lawyer; head of the Central Guoshu Institute editorial department from 1930; published
  《少林武当考》 (1931) against both the Bodhidharma-Shaolin and Zhang Sanfeng myths: "Chinese
  martial arts were already highly developed in ancient times, and there is no reason to
  fabricate myths about Bodhidharma and Zhang Sanfeng." Then did field research at Chen Village.
  Wile: "Tang Hao's studies managed to anger both camps." (Sequencing trap: the 1931 book
  slightly PREDATES the field trip - do not say the trip produced the book.)
- Even the consensus is contested: 21 August 2007 the General Administration of Sport gave Chen
  Village a plaque naming it "the birthplace of taijiquan"; two months later protest forced its
  removal.

**Wile on the classics' provenance.** The received canon exists in writing only from the
mid-to-late 19th century, inside one family circle in 永年 Yongnian: 武澄清 Wu Chengqing (b.
c.1800, magistrate of 舞阳县 Wuyang 1852), 武禹襄 Wu Yuxiang (1812-1880), 李亦畬 Li Yiyu
(1832-1892). Wile calls them "the Wu family 'salt shop' texts."
- The crux, in Wile's words - one of "three gaps in the record" is "how to account for the
  paucity of written theory in the Chen family and its richness in Wu and Yang."
- **The Salt Shop story is a claim, not a fact** - that Wu Chengqing found Wang Zongyue's texts
  in a 盐店 salt shop rests on Li Yiyu's own assertion. A provenance story told by the family
  that benefited from it.
- **Do NOT say "Wile proved Wang Zongyue never existed."** Wile: "Wang Zongyue is a very
  contested figure... It is critical to the Daoist case that he be considered a Ming figure and
  equally critical to the Chen Village and Tangcun cases that he is dated Qing." Chinese
  reference works say 王宗岳生平不可考 "his life cannot be verified."
- Wile on the politics: "For traditionalists, attacking the Zhang Sanfeng creation theory is
  tantamount to committing cultural treason." "In today's martial arts marketplace, the various
  schools and styles vie to become commercial conquerors by inventing their histories."

**1956 simplified 24-form 二十四式简化太极拳.** Commissioned by the State Physical Culture and
Sports Commission 国家体委. Based on Yang style (chosen for 流传最广、适应性强 widest circulation,
greatest adaptability). Design principles 简单、明确、易学. China's first unified martial arts
textbook from a state sports authority. Runs ~5-6 minutes.
- HEDGE the committee membership (two lists circulate). The striking claim IF used: Chinese
  sources place **Tang Hao** on the 1955 research group - the man who demythologised taijiquan
  sitting on the committee that standardised it.
- HEDGE "most practiced form in the world": almost certainly true, **no survey exists**. Say so.
  What IS citable: Chinese state media at the UNESCO listing reported taijiquan in 150+ countries
  with 100M+ practitioners (state media, not an independent count).
- Do NOT state the 88-form (1957) or 42-form (1989) dates - unverified.

**UNESCO 2020.** Element name is exactly **"Taijiquan"**, not "Tai Chi". Inscribed 2020 at the
**fifteenth session (15.COM)**, 14-18 December 2020, announced 17 December. Representative List
of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Submitted by China. Element **no. 00424**.
- **Teachable point: UNESCO's official text endorses the 17th-century Wenxian origin - the Tang
  Hao line, not the Zhang Sanfeng line. The myth lost at the UN.**

### Session 2: rooting, the lab

**双重 shuāngzhòng is the classical term, and it names a FAULT.** Wang Zongyue's 太极拳论 says
only: **偏沉则随，双重则滞** *piān chén zé suí, shuāng zhòng zé zhì* - "sink to one side and you
can follow; be doubly weighted and you stagnate."
- **CRITICAL FOR THE LAB:** "single-weightedness 单重" is a LATER derived term - it belongs to
  the Yang family 《太极法说》 manuscript tradition (c.1875, section 太極輕重浮沉解), which is
  what Wile translated in *T'ai-chi Touchstones* (1983) and *Lost T'ai-chi Classics* (1996).
- **THE PERCENTAGES (100/0, 70/30, 60/40) APPEAR IN NO CLASSICAL TEXT.** They are modern teaching
  conventions and vary by school and lineage - Yang commonly ~70/30 in the bow stance, Wu style
  often 100/0, and sources disagree even within a style. **The lab must say this out loud.**
- Yang Chengfu's Essential 4 states the principle without numbers: shift onto one leg, that leg
  is 实 shí solid, the other 虚 xū empty; only then are turns "light, nimble and almost without
  effort."

**The rooting line.** 其根在脚，发于腿，主宰于腰，形于手指 *qí gēn zài jiǎo, fā yú tuǐ, zhǔzǎi yú
yāo, xíng yú shǒuzhǐ* - "the root is in the feet, issued through the legs, governed by the waist,
expressed in the fingers." HEDGE precise attribution: circulates within 武禹襄《十三势行功心解》/
《打手要言》 and inside editions of 《太极拳经》 attributed to Zhang Sanfeng. Attribute to "the
classics" generally, not one named author.

**沉 chén sinking** is embedded in the Ten Essentials: Essential 2 含胸拔背 links containing the
chest to 气沉丹田 "sinking the breath to the dantian"; Essential 5 is 沉肩坠肘.

### Session 3: 松 song and the body method

**松 sōng / 放松 fàngsōng** - loose, released. Releasing unnecessary holding in joints and muscles
while KEEPING structure. Not limpness.
- Classical anchor for "not collapse," from 十三勢行工心解: **极柔软，然后极坚刚** *jí róuruǎn,
  ránhòu jí jiāngāng* - "extremely soft, and only then extremely hard." Softness is the
  precondition of firmness, not its absence.
- Yang Chengfu on why stiffness is the problem (Essential 5): "If you lift your shoulders, the
  qi will rise with them and the whole body will be without strength."
- HEDGE "why it is hardest for beginners" - practitioner consensus only, no institutional source.
  The teaching image is "iron wrapped in cotton"; 松 is paired with 掤 péng, outward supportive
  structure.

**Yang Chengfu's Ten Essentials 太极拳十要.** Orally transmitted by 杨澄甫 Yang Chengfu
(1883-1936), written down by his student 陈微明 Chen Weiming, **first published 1925** in Chen
Weiming's 《太极拳术》; later in Yang Chengfu's 《太极拳体用全书》 (1934). HEDGE: Chen Weiming
positions himself as recorder, but likely reworked the lectures.

**Verified standard order** (many casual lists get this wrong):
| # | 汉字 | Pinyin | Plain English |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 虚灵顶劲 | xū líng dǐng jìn | empty, lively energy at the crown; head suspended, neck not stiff |
| 2 | 含胸拔背 | hán xiōng bá bèi | contain the chest, pluck up the back (enables 气沉丹田) |
| 3 | 松腰 | sōng yāo | loosen the waist ("the commander of the whole body") |
| 4 | 分虚实 | fēn xū shí | distinguish empty and full ("the number one rule") |
| 5 | 沉肩坠肘 | chén jiān zhuì zhǒu | sink the shoulders, drop the elbows |
| 6 | 用意不用力 | yòng yì bù yòng lì | use intent, not brute force |
| 7 | 上下相随 | shàng xià xiāng suí | upper and lower body follow each other |
| 8 | 内外相合 | nèi wài xiāng hé | inner and outer unite |
| 9 | 相连不断 | xiāng lián bù duàn | linked together without interruption |
| 10 | 动中求静 | dòng zhōng qiú jìng | seek stillness within movement |
Common structural reading: 1-5 body mechanics, 6-10 more abstract.

**用意不用力 - NOT from the classics.** Yang Chengfu, Essential 6, 1925 - roughly 45-60 years
LATER than the Wu/Li classics, a different textual layer. His own explanation is concrete and
TCM-physiological, not mystical: "Don't employ even the tiniest amount of coarse strength, which
would cause musculoskeletal or circulatory blockage." "If you move the body about with stiff
force, you swamp the meridians. Chi and blood are impeded." The conceptual ancestor in the
classics is a different phrase - 意气君来骨肉臣 "intent and qi are sovereign; bone and flesh are
subject" (十三势行工歌诀) - a paraphrase relationship, not a source.

### Session 4: the 13 postures + push hands

**Structure: 八门 eight gates + 五步 five steps = 十三势.** UNESCO uses exactly this framing.
十三势 is also an older name for the art itself.

Eight gates: 掤 péng ward-off (buoyant outward support) · 捋 lǚ roll-back (leading force past) ·
挤 jǐ press (squeezing forward) · 按 àn push/press-down · 采 cǎi pluck (sharp downward grab) ·
挒 liè split/rend (two opposing forces) · 肘 zhǒu elbow · 靠 kào shoulder/body bump.
- **Pronunciation trap:** 掤 reads *péng* only in taijiquan jargon; in dictionaries it is **bīng**
  (a quiver lid). Cheng Man-ching noted exactly this.

Five steps: 进 jìn advance · 退 tuì retreat · 左顾 zuǒ gù look left · 右盼 yòu pàn gaze right ·
中定 zhōng dìng central equilibrium.

**THE CORRESPONDENCES ARE UNSTABLE - teach them as varying overlays, not doctrine.** The 1881 Li
Yiyu manuscript reads: 掤捋擠按。即坎離震兑。四正方也。採挒肘靠。即乾坤艮巽。四斜角也。進步退步
左顧右盻中定。即金木水火土也。 So: four primaries → four cardinal trigrams 坎离震兑; four
secondaries → four corner trigrams 乾坤艮巽; five steps → 金木水火土.
- **Discrepancy 1:** many modern sources give 掤=乾, 捋=坤 etc. The difference tracks 先天八卦
  pre-heaven vs 后天八卦 post-heaven trigram arrangements. No single canonical mapping.
- **Discrepancy 2:** read positionally the classic gives 进=金, 退=木, 左顾=水, 右盼=火, 中定=土.
  But the 《清代杨氏传抄老谱》 Qing Yang-family manuscript gives the directional-cosmology mapping:
  **进=火 (south), 退=水 (north), 左顾=木 (east), 右盼=金 (west), 中定=土 (centre)** - and THIS
  second version is what almost everyone teaches.
- Honest line: the eight-gate/five-step structure is stable and old; the trigram and five-phase
  correspondences are mnemonic overlays that vary between texts and schools.

**推手 tuīshǒu push hands.** A partner SENSITIVITY exercise; UNESCO lists it as a core component.
Academic definition: "The aim of Tuishou practice is to sensitize oneself to an opponent's
movement and intention while concealing one's own"; non-competitive tuishou is "a collaborative
somatic listening application" (Gómez-Lozano et al, *Frontiers in Communication* 2022;7:983290).
Three energies: 听劲 tīng jìn listening, 化劲 huà jìn transforming, 懂劲 dǒng jìn understanding.
Strategy: 粘连黏随 zhān lián nián suí - stick, connect, adhere, follow.
- **NOT sparring, and NOT standard in a beginner health class.** Harvard's typical class is
  warm-up → form → qigong breathing; push hands is not mentioned.

**The classics' famous lines** (from the 1881 Li Yiyu MS, cross-checked against zh.wikisource):

(a) From 太极拳论: **一羽不能加，蝇虫不能落。** *yī yǔ bù néng jiā, yíng chóng bù néng luò* - "A
single feather cannot be added; a fly or insect cannot alight." Context matters - the next clause
completes it: **人不知我，我独知人** "the opponent does not know me; I alone know the opponent."

(b) **四两拨千斤 is from 《打手歌》, NOT 太极拳论.** The Treatise QUOTES it as an existing saying:
察四两拨千斤之句，显非力胜 - "examining the line 'four taels deflect a thousand catties,' it is
clear this is not victory by strength." The word 句 jù ("the line") is decisive.
- Full 打手歌: 掤捋挤按须认真，上下相随人难进。任他巨力来打我，牵动四两拨千斤。引进落空合即出，
  粘连黏随不丢顶。
- **The full form is 牵动四两拨千斤 - "DRAW HIM INTO MOTION, and four taels deflect a thousand
  catties." The 牵动 is the mechanism, not decoration. Without first pulling the opponent off
  balance, four taels deflect nothing. The single most-mangled line in English tai chi writing.**
- Units: 两 liǎng = tael, 斤 jīn = catty, 1 jin = 16 liang, so the ratio is 1:4,000. A late-Qing
  jin ≈ 596.8 g, so literally "four taels (~150 g) deflect a thousand catties (~600 kg)."
  "Four ounces moves a thousand pounds" is right in spirit, wrong in units.

(c) From 太极拳论: **本是舍己从人，多误舍近求远。** *běn shì shě jǐ cóng rén* - "It is
fundamentally a matter of abandoning oneself and following the other; most err by abandoning what
is near and seeking what is far." HEDGE: 舍己从人 appears in more than one text.

**The canon and its attribution.** Best primary witness = Li Yiyu's 1881 handwritten manual to
Hao He, containing: 太极拳论 (attr. Wang Zongyue) · 十三势行工歌诀 (anon.) · 打手要言 (Wu Yuxiang)
· 打手歌 (anon.) · 太极拳小序 (Li Yiyu - the "I do not know the origin" text) · 五字诀 (Li Yiyu).
- Naming trap: the same opening text circulates as 《太极拳经》/Zhang Sanfeng, as "Zhang Sanfeng's
  太极拳论," and inside Wu Yuxiang's 打手要言. Chinese sources concede 实际作者有待考究.
- Honest one-sentence position: all four canonical attributions are known only from 19th-century
  manuscripts produced inside one family circle in Yongnian, who are at minimum the compilers,
  editors and attributors, and possibly the authors of much of it. The texts acquired 经
  classic/scripture status only from Guan Baiyi's 1912 edition onward. Wile: "Taijiquan
  practitioners may fairly be called 'a people of the book'."

### Session 5: the evidence

**Falls prevention - the strongest case.** Sherrington C, Fairhall NJ, Wallbank GK, et al.
"Exercise for preventing falls in older people living in the community." **Cochrane Database of
Systematic Reviews 2019;1(1):CD012424.** 108 RCTs, 23,407 community-dwelling participants,
25 countries.
- **Tai chi, rate of falls: RaR 0.81 (95% CI 0.67-0.99), 2,655 participants, 7 studies,
  LOW-certainty evidence → a 19% reduction.**
- **Tai chi, number of people who fall: RR 0.80 (95% CI 0.70-0.91), 2,677 participants, 8
  studies, HIGH-certainty evidence → a 20% reduction.**
- **The honest comparison:** general balance and functional exercise did slightly better on fall
  rate - RaR 0.76 (95% CI 0.70-0.81), 7,920 participants, 39 studies, HIGH certainty; fallers
  RR 0.87 (95% CI 0.82-0.91), 8,288 participants, 37 studies, high certainty. **Tai chi is one
  good option among several, not a uniquely powerful one.**

**Guidelines - who names it:**
- **YES, by name: World Falls Guidelines 2022** (Montero-Odasso et al, *Age and Ageing*
  2022;51(9):afac205), verbatim: "We recommend inclusion, when feasible, of Tai Chi and/or
  additional individualised progressive resistance strength training." **GRADE 1B** (strong
  recommendation, moderate-quality evidence).
- **YES, strongly: ACR / Arthritis Foundation 2019 guideline STRONGLY RECOMMENDS tai chi for
  knee and hip osteoarthritis** (Kolasinski et al). The strongest clinical guideline endorsement
  tai chi has.
- **NO, not by name: USPSTF 2024** (*JAMA*, 2 July 2024) recommends exercise interventions
  (B recommendation) for adults 65+ at increased risk but **did not identify one exercise type as
  superior** and does not single out tai chi.
- **Indirect: CDC** - via the named programme **Tai Ji Quan: Moving for Better Balance (TJQMBB)**,
  developed by Fuzhong Li, recognised by CDC/ACL/NCOA as a top-tier evidence-based falls
  programme. Do NOT claim a STEADI document names tai chi in its own recommendation text.
- **NOT VERIFIED: UK NICE. Do not claim it.**

**Fibromyalgia - the best head-to-head in the field.** Wang C, Schmid CH, Fielding RA, et al.
"Effect of tai chi versus aerobic exercise for fibromyalgia." **BMJ 2018;360:k851.** 52-week
single-blind comparative effectiveness RCT. **n=226** (151 tai chi across four dose arms, 75
supervised aerobic exercise). Primary outcome FIQR at 24 weeks. **Combined tai chi groups improved
more: difference 5.5 points (95% CI 0.6-10.4, P=0.03).** Conclusion: "Tai chi mind-body treatment
results in similar or greater improvement in symptoms than aerobic exercise, the current most
commonly prescribed non-drug treatment."
- Earlier: Wang C et al, **NEJM 2010;363(8):743-754**, n=66, tai chi vs wellness education +
  stretching, 60 min twice weekly x 12 weeks. FIQ between-group difference **-18.4, P<0.001**,
  maintained at 24 weeks. HEDGE: small, and the comparator was a low-dose active control. The
  2018 trial is stronger precisely because it compared against a real exercise dose.

**Parkinson's.** Li F, Harmer P, Fitzgerald K, et al. **NEJM 2012;366(6):511-519.** **n=195**,
Hoehn and Yahr stages 1-4, **three arms: tai chi vs resistance training vs stretching**, 60 min
twice weekly x 24 weeks. Limits-of-stability outcomes:
- vs resistance training: maximum excursion **+5.55 points (95% CI 1.12-9.97)**, directional
  control **+10.45 (95% CI 3.89-17.00)**.
- vs stretching: maximum excursion **+11.98 (95% CI 7.21-16.74)**, directional control **+11.38
  (95% CI 5.50-17.27)**.
- **A genuine head-to-head: tai chi beat resistance training, not just stretching.** Unusual in
  this literature.

**Other conditions** (NCCIH review-level): knee OA - low-to-moderate strength evidence of benefit,
plus the ACR strong recommendation. COPD - may improve exercise capacity, lung function, QoL vs
no treatment, but "unlikely to be any more effective than other types of exercise (moderate to
low certainty)." Cognition - promising 2023 findings, review-level certainty not established.
Blood pressure - 2020 review favourable "though studies were of poor quality"; best single trial
is *JAMA Network Open* Feb 2024, n=342, 12 months, tai chi vs aerobic: office SBP difference
**-2.40 mm Hg (95% CI -4.39 to -0.41, P=0.02)**, nighttime ambulatory SBP **-4.08 (95% CI -6.59
to -1.57, P=0.002)**, BP normalised in 21.8% vs 15.6%. Statistically significant, clinically
modest, single trial, conducted in China. Cancer - "promising," no conclusions. Stroke - gait
improved short-term, balance did not; studies small and high risk of bias.

**THE LIMITS - the crucial section.** Yang G-Y, Hunter J, Bu F-L, et al. "Determining the safety
and effectiveness of Tai Chi: a critical overview of 210 systematic reviews of controlled clinical
trials." **Systematic Reviews 2022;11:260.** 210 reviews screened, 47 synthesised, **114 effect
estimates from 37 reviews covering 59,306 adults.**
- **GRADE certainty: high 8 estimates (7.0%); moderate 43 (37.7%); low 36 (31.6%); very low 27
  (23.7%). So 93% of effect estimates in the entire tai chi literature are below high certainty.**
- Downgrade reasons: risk of bias 92 estimates (80.7%), imprecision 43 (37.7%), inconsistency 37
  (32.5%).
- **AMSTAR-2 quality of the reviews themselves (n=47): high 2, moderate 7, low 20, critically low
  18 - more than 80% low or critically low.**
- **THE KEY HONEST QUESTION - is tai chi better than equivalent other exercise? Mostly no, it is
  comparable.** Effects were greatest vs no intervention or health education, smaller vs
  medication, smallest vs other exercise. **Effect sizes shrink markedly when the comparator is a
  real dose of other exercise.** The genuine exceptions where tai chi beat an active comparator:
  Wang 2018 BMJ (vs aerobic, fibromyalgia, +5.5 FIQR), Li 2012 NEJM (vs resistance training,
  Parkinson's balance), JAMA Netw Open 2024 (vs aerobic, prehypertension, -2.40 mm Hg). Three
  trials, all modest margins.
- **Defensible summary: tai chi is a genuinely effective form of exercise with a few areas of
  demonstrated edge over comparable exercise. It is not established as categorically superior to
  exercise in general.**
- HEDGE the "94.1% of studies positive" bibliometric figure - search-snippet sourced.
- No tai chi trial reporting guideline exists. Do not claim one.

**The qi question.** Neither NCCIH's tai chi page nor its TCM page uses 气 qì or 经络 meridians as
an explanatory mechanism; where NCCIH describes mechanism it uses biomedical language. Meanwhile
the practice's own framework is fully present in its primary sources - Yang Chengfu's Essential 6
says "If you move the body about with stiff force, you swamp the meridians." UNESCO cites "theories
of traditional Chinese medicine" as a formative influence. Wayne's eight ingredients is the standard
research workaround - no qi, no meridians, each component measurable in principle.
- **Honest line: the movements, breathing, attention and social setting are testable and are being
  tested; qi and meridians are the tradition's own descriptive vocabulary and are not what the
  trials measure. Neither fact undermines the other. Keep the two registers visibly separate rather
  than translating one into the other.**

**Safety.** Yang 2022: "No significant differences were found in the risk of serious, non-serious,
or intervention-related adverse events from Tai Chi compared to both physically active and inactive
interventions." NCCIH: "Tai chi appears to be safe"; reported adverse effects "were minor, such as
musculoskeletal aches and pain." VA: "generally thought to be safe," possible minor knee pain;
beginners should start in a classroom format "to ensure safety and good technique." HEDGE the
who-should-ask-a-doctor lines (Mayo, Arthritis Foundation both block automated fetching).

### Session 6: the styles + finding a teacher

**The five families** (HEDGE the whole table: descriptions come from lineage organisations, not
peer-reviewed scholarship; founders and dates are well attested, characteristics are conventional):

| Style | Founder | Dates | Lineage in | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 陈 Chen | 陈王廷 Chen Wangting | 17th c (see hedge) | oldest; Chen Village | 缠丝劲 silk-reeling spiral force; alternating slow and explosive 发劲; low stances |
| 杨 Yang | 杨露禅 Yang Luchan; standardised by 杨澄甫 Yang Chengfu | 1799-1872; YCF 1883-1936 | Chen Changxing | 大架 large frame, even tempo; YCF REMOVED fajin, jumping, stamping; most popular worldwide |
| 吴 Wu | 吴全佑 Wu Quanyou; 吴鉴泉 Wu Jianquan | 1834-1902; 1870-1942 | Yang Luchan and 杨班侯 Yang Banhou | compact small-circle hands; characteristic slight forward inclination |
| 武(郝) Wu-Hao | 武禹襄 Wu Yuxiang; spread by 郝为真 Hao Weizhen | 1812-1880; Hao 1849-1920 | ~10 yrs Yang Luchan, then ~1 month 陈清平 Chen Qingping | small frame, very internal; strict empty/full distinction; 开合 open-close |
| 孙 Sun | 孙禄堂 Sun Lutang | 1860-1933 | 郝为真 (1912) + his own xingyi and bagua | syncretic; high stances; agile following step; omits crouching, leaping, fajin |

- **THE ROMANIZATION TRAP:** two entirely different families both romanize "Wu" - **吳 Wú**
  (Wu Quanyou/Wu Jianquan, from Yang Banhou) and **武 Wǔ** (Wu Yuxiang of Yongnian). Different
  character, tone, founder, lineage, body method. The 武 style is written **Wu(Hao)** to
  disambiguate, honouring Hao Weizhen.
- **"Five" is a convention, not a closed set.** 和式 He style (from 赵堡 Zhaobao, named for 和兆元
  He Zhaoyuan) is often counted sixth; the Chinese Wushu Association duanwei syllabus covers Chen,
  Yang, Wu, Wu(Hao), Sun AND He.
- Chen sub-frames: Chen Changxing (1771-1853) 老架 Old Frame; 陈有本 Chen Youben (1780-1858) 小架
  Small Frame; 陈清平 Chen Qingping (1795-1868) at Zhaobao; **陈发科 Chen Fake (1887-1957)** brought
  Chen style to Beijing.
- HEDGE Sun Lutang's 《太极拳学》 year (1921 per Wikipedia, 1919 in some sources).

**Finding a teacher - the certification reality.**
- **NCCIH VERBATIM: "Tai chi instructors don't have to be licensed, and the practice isn't
  regulated by the Federal Government or individual states. There's no national standard for tai
  chi certification."** NCCIH elsewhere: "various tai chi organizations offer training and
  certification programs with differing criteria."
- **Global governing body? Only for sport.** The International Wushu Federation (IWUF) governs
  competitive wushu including taijiquan as an event, and runs the World Taijiquan Championships.
  **It licenses nothing about teaching.** An IWUF connection says something about competitive
  pedigree and nothing about competence with older or unwell students.
- HEDGE: Chinese Wushu Association 段位 duanwei grading is real but domestic and sport-oriented
  (nine duan in three bands, 23 styles, 629 test centres - 2013 secondary reporting). A Western
  beginner cannot easily verify a claimed duan.
- UK: **Tai Chi Union for Great Britain** - ~1,000 members, 800+ instructors; instructor applicants
  need **at least 5 consecutive years of practice** and are assessed on common principles rather
  than style specifics. **A voluntary membership body, not a statutory regulator**; one-time panel
  assessment plus subscription, not ongoing supervision. Non-membership is not evidence of a bad
  teacher.
- US: no national body. Nearest analogue is programme-specific: **Tai Chi for Health Institute**
  (Dr Paul Lam) certifies instructors in ITS OWN named programmes, requiring an authorised
  workshop, **renewal every two years**, and **first aid / CPR**. Scope caveat: certifies delivery
  of a specific curriculum, not "qualified tai chi teacher" generally - but the renewal cycle and
  CPR requirement are more accountability than most tai chi credentials carry.
- **The vetting checklist an authoritative body actually endorses:** NCCIH, "6 Things To Know When
  Selecting a Complementary Health Practitioner." The key item is NOT "what's your certification"
  but: explain all your health conditions and **ask about the practitioner's training and
  experience working with people who have your conditions** - "Choose a practitioner who
  understands how to work with people with your specific needs, even if general well-being is your
  goal." Also: get names via your doctor or a hospital; ask whether they will work alongside your
  conventional providers; tell all your providers what you are doing.
- **On lineage:** it is the traditional authority claim, filling the space a licence would occupy.
  Because no regulator exists, there is no register, no adjudicating body and no penalty for
  overstating a connection - to a newcomer a lineage claim is unfalsifiable. **Neither NCCIH,
  Harvard, Mayo nor VA mentions lineage at all in getting-started guidance; they care about safety,
  adaptation, and experience with your condition. That silence is itself the signal.**

**Class facts** (Harvard Health, free article): structure is **warm-up → form instruction →
qigong breathing**. "Most beginning programs and tai chi interventions tested in medical research
last at least 12 weeks, with instruction once or twice a week and practice at home." 60 minutes is
the research standard. Loose clothing. **Barefoot or thin, flexible shoes** - soles thin enough to
feel the ground; **running shoes are "usually unsuitable"** (thick cushioning and a raised heel
both interfere with ground feel and weight-shifting). No equipment. Seated and wheelchair variants
are normal (VA). Push hands is not standard beginner content.

**Realistic timeline - the best anchor in the whole map.** **TJQMBB**, the CDC/ACL/NCOA-recognised
top-tier falls programme, is "derived from a contemporary routine known as Simplified 24-Form Tai
Ji Quan" and consists of an **8-FORM core** - delivered as **two one-hour sessions weekly for 24
weeks.** **If researchers cut the 24-form down to 8 movements and still allocated 24 weeks, "learn
tai chi in 8 weeks" describes memorising a sequence, not acquiring a skill.**
- Tai Chi for Arthritis and Fall Prevention: 8 weeks x 2 sessions, or 16 weeks x 1 - **16 hour-long
  sessions total**.
- HEDGE "8-12 weeks to learn the 24-form" - school and commercial sources only, no institutional
  or peer-reviewed source states a learning duration. Label as practitioner consensus.
- **The genuinely reassuring, genuinely sourced message: every trial in Session 5 measured real
  outcomes at 12-24 weeks in participants who had learned only abbreviated material. You do not
  need to finish a form to get the documented benefit.**

**Free resources, verified live:** NCCIH "Tai Chi: What You Need To Know" (best single starting
point) · NCCIH "Qigong" · NCCIH "6 Things To Know When Selecting a Complementary Health
Practitioner" (the vetting checklist) · VA Whole Health Tai Chi and Qigong pages + VA News "Live
Whole Health #300: Cloud Hands" (an 18-minute follow-along led by a VA recreation therapist and
certified instructor - the best free government-produced video found) + "#253: Adaptive Tai Chi"
(seated) · Harvard Health free articles ("The Health Benefits of Tai Chi", "The active ingredients
of tai chi") · NCOA programme pages (TJQMBB, Tai Chi for Arthritis, Evidence-Based Falls Prevention
Programs) · Douglas Wile "Fighting Words," *Martial Arts Studies* 4 (2017), open access · Brennan
Translation "The Taiji Classics" (bilingual 1881 manuscript) · UNESCO element 00424.

## Must-hedge list (carry into every page)
1. Chen Wangting's dates - say "17th century."
2. Tang Hao's birth year and field-trip date; the 1931 book predates the trip.
3. Who created the 1956 form - two committee lists circulate.
4. "Most practiced form in the world" - almost certainly true, no survey exists.
5. "100 million practitioners" - Chinese state media, not an independent count.
6. 88-form and 42-form dates - unverified, do not state.
7. Style characteristics - lineage descriptions, not measured facts.
8. Whether Wang Zongyue existed - Wile is deliberately non-committal; that IS his position.
9. Which classical text a famous line "really" belongs to - attributions are the contested object.
10. Trigram and five-phase correspondences for the 13 postures - genuinely unstable.
11. **Weight percentages (70/30 etc) - modern pedagogy, absent from the classics. The lab says so.**
12. 单重 "single-weightedness" - later 太极法说 layer (c.1875), not Wang Zongyue.
13. Why 松 is hard for beginners - practitioner consensus only.
14. What 用意不用力 means in practice - interpretive.
15. Fear of falling - no verified effect size, do not quantify.
16. Cognition - promising, review-level certainty not established.
17. Blood pressure - single trial, modest margin, conducted in China.
18. NICE - unverified. STEADI - indirect only, via named programmes.
19. Movement counts in the Wang and Li trials - not stated in the abstracts.
20. "8-12 weeks to learn the 24-form" - practitioner consensus, pair with the TJQMBB anchor.
